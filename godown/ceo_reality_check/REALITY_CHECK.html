The 1 Billion LoC Reality Check

A forensic read of every deal, stage move and note in the LH2 CRM — testing whether the 1-billion-lines-of-code target is reachable, what weekly rate the evidence actually supports, and where the data itself cannot be trusted.

2,141 deals · 4,921 stage transitions · 1,981 notes · 288 tasks · 34 days of history
HubSpot portal 246754894 · compiled 17 Aug 2026 · six independent analyses, then three adversarial reviews

Probability of reaching 1 Billion Tier-1 LoC through this funnel
2% – 25%

Not impossible, but unlikely — and the spread is not noise. It is one specific unknown: whether we can keep re-harvesting the same companies. At the one-shot assumption the probability is 1.6%; if repeat harvesting scales as it already has, 24.5%. Monte Carlo median ceiling: 344M Tier-1 LoC, 95% interval 98M – 920M.

Read this before any number below.

The audit found that the CRM does not record when codebases were acquired — it records when someone updated the CRM. All 11 wins were clicked in five sessions, four of them within 16 seconds of each other. And 70% of all won LoC was bulk-imported backlog, not funnel output. Every weekly rate in the original version of this report was measuring data entry. The figures below are rebuilt around that.

01 What the data actually is

This section exists because three independent adversarial reviews all landed on the same place: the headline series was not measuring what it appeared to measure. Both defects are confirmed directly against the raw export.

On the 15–20M/week hypothesis — the honest answer is "top edge, on raw"
  • Measured in raw LoC, 15M/week sits at the very top of the honest central estimate (11.3–16.3M), not in the middle of it.
  • 20M raw/week has roughly a 1-in-5 chance (P ≈ 0.196) of being the true long-run rate.
  • Measured in Tier-1 LoC — the CEO's own quality bar — the equivalent band is only 6.7–9.7M/week. Tier-1 and raw differ by 1.675×, and the two have been used interchangeably.
So: the instinct that "15–20M is achievable and not more" is directionally sound but optimistic. It is a reasonable raw stretch target and an unreachable Tier-1 one. Fix the unit before fixing the number.

The biggest leak is the list, not the selling

966 deals — 45.1% of the entire database — died because the list was wrong
  • Wrong fit: 727 · Wrong number: 239 — together 82.9% of all cold-call deaths
  • Genuinely not interested: 199 — only 17.1%
Five out of every six cold-call deaths are a data-quality failure, not a prospect saying no. The callers are not losing these deals; they were never callable. Fixing qualification upstream is worth more than any change to how the calls are made — and it is the one lever that raises win rate without lowering LoC per win.

The cleanest single test of the hypothesisEven the best week this company has ever recorded — 33 scripts shared — implies only 12.56M Tier-1 LoC/week. A 15M Tier-1 target requires 39.4 scripts/week, a level never once achieved, against a current run of 5. On a raw basis the peak week does clear 21M, which is why 15–20M feels right when raw and Tier-1 are used interchangeably. The unit is doing all the work in that hypothesis.

A reporting defect worth fixing

21 of the 62 deals that died after reaching Script Shared (34%) carry a cold-call death stage — Dead/ColdCall/WrongFit or Dead/ColdCall/Not Interested. Reps are reusing early-stage dead reasons for late-stage losses, which means any loss-reason analysis built on stage labels understates late-stage failure and overstates list quality problems. The 45% list-quality figure above is, if anything, conservative.

07 What to do

1
Fix the measurement before the targetRecord an actual acquisition date separate from the CRM click, and tag migrated deals so imported backlog never again counts as funnel output. Until this is fixed no weekly rate can be trusted — including the ones in this report.
2
Decide raw or Tier-1, and say whichThey differ by 1.675×. "1 Billion" means two very different programmes depending on the answer, and today both are in use.
3
Measure and target repeat harvest (R)R = 1.50 today, entirely unmanaged, and it is the difference between a 1.6% and 24.5% chance of the target. Backspace gave three harvests and Serpent two. Make R an owned metric and work the existing won accounts.
4
Fix the list before fixing the calling966 deals (45% of the database) died on wrong-fit or wrong-number — five of every six cold-call deaths. This is the only lever that raises win rate without lowering LoC per win, and it is upstream qualification, not sales technique.
5
Unblock scripts, not dials16 scripts/week and falling while leads grew 6.5×. Scripts gate 8 of 11 wins. Find the constraint and clear the ~660-lead backlog.
6
Hold lead creation flatSupply (721/wk) already exceeds consumption (~608/wk). More sourcing adds backlog and accelerates universe burn.
7
Reopen the negotiation losses13.6M Tier-1 LoC died on price after all acquisition cost was already spent. Highest return available per hour of effort.
8
Protect against caller churn100% turnover in 4.9 weeks; 5–6 working days to ramp; 12.8× spread in per-seat output. Retention beats recruitment here.
9
Take the universe question to the boardEven with repeat harvesting, one billion needs several times the companies we can name. Expanding beyond Indian IT-services is a strategy decision, not an execution one.

08 Honest caveats

  • Everything rests on 9 Tier-1 wins across 6 distinct companies in a 34-day-old CRM. That is the real sample size — deal-level counts overstate it, because Backspace and Serpent are one relationship each.
  • Two-thirds of the won LoC predates the funnel. The migration means we are partly measuring a Google Sheet, not a sales motion.
  • Close dates are unreliable, so every cycle-time and weekly-velocity figure carries an unknown offset. The "zero closes since 10 Aug" may mean nothing at all.
  • Deliqt alone is 42% of Tier-1 LoC won. Remove it and the picture changes materially.
  • Only 16 of 2,141 deals carry a LoC value. If LoC is under-recorded on wins, the per-win figure is understated and the outlook improves.
  • No lead-source difference is statistically significant — every pairwise Fisher exact test returns p ≥ 0.19. Do not reallocate budget between sources on this evidence alone.
  • Loss reasons are unreliable at depth — 34% of deals that died after Script Shared carry a cold-call death stage, so stage-based loss analysis is distorted.
  • R = 1.50 is censored upward, measured over five weeks on still-open relationships. It could be materially higher — which is exactly why it should be measured deliberately rather than inferred.

Method — six independent analyses over the same dataset (funnel conversion, LoC/PR economics, human capacity, source quality, velocity and pipeline, universe sizing), a constraint model, then three adversarial reviews under statistical, steel-man and data-integrity lenses. All three refuted the first model; their corrections are incorporated above and were re-verified directly against the raw export.
